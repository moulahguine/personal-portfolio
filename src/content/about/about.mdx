## How it started

Hi, I'm Mohamed, a frontend developer from Morocco, currently studying Software Engineering in Türkiye.

![Between where I came from and where I hope to go.](../../assets/images/about/myimage.jpeg)

My relationship with computers started long before I knew what programming was. In 2011, my father brought home a computer. Back then, having one at home was rare where I lived, so I was fascinated. I wasn't interested in software yet; I just wanted to see what was inside. I would open it up, look at the parts, collect pieces I could find, and try to fix things that didn't work. I often had no idea what I was doing, but that never stopped me from trying again. For a long time, I was all about hardware.

</section>

<section id="discovering-programming">

## My first steps in programming

In Morocco, we didn't really have programming classes in school before high school. My first real chance to learn programming came in my first year of high school. We started with Scratch, an easy way to begin. Instead of writing lines of code, we used blocks to make things happen. I loved it. One small memory from that time still stands out to me.

One day, our teacher asked:

> Does anyone know which language Android was built with?

I said:

> Java.

I was probably the only one in class with an answer. I wasn't even sure, but I had seen 'Java' on my phone when it started up, so I guessed it was Java. She was happy, clapped, and said a few nice things. For a few minutes, I thought I was a genius.

Looking back, it was a small moment. But experiences like that made programming interesting for me. I started paying more attention to software, and before I finished high school, I knew I wanted to become a software engineer.

</section>

After high school, I started with frontend development. My first real lessons came from [**Elzero Web School**](https://elzero.org), where I learned **HTML**, **CSS**, and **JavaScript**. HTML was okay, but CSS was tough. As a beginner, I struggled to make things look right. I'd change one thing, and something else would move unexpectedly. JavaScript was even harder.

But I was lucky to learn from someone who explained things well. [**Osama Elzero**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/osamaelzero/) made difficult topics easy to understand, and the exercises and challenges pushed me to practice what I was learning rather than just watch the lessons.

I really want to say thank you here.

I don't want to forget the people who helped me start. His explanations made a big difference, and the exercises he gave us kept me practicing. Even now, I remember how much [**Osama Elzero**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/osamaelzero/) ro helped me.

So, thank you.

<section id="first-client">

## Getting my first client

After studying for a while, I wanted to find ways to use what I was learning. My first client found me through social media. He ran a business helping students with university applications, but his website was outdated and in need of work. At the same time, I needed help with my own university admission.

So we made a deal.

He agreed to help me with my university admission, and I worked on his website. That was my first client. It's not the kind of first-client story you read in business books, but it worked for both of us.

Since then, I've built all kinds of projects, especially e-commerce sites, landing pages, and dashboards. E-commerce caught my interest because I wanted to see how everything worked behind the scenes. I became curious about how the different parts connected and how products and data moved around. The more I worked on these projects, the more I wanted to learn about what happens beyond the frontend.

</section>

After about a year of learning frontend development and freelancing, I decided to go to university to study Software Engineering. It wasn't a hard decision. I had wanted to do this for a long time and learn it the right way. I'm still learning, of course.

<section id="software-values">

## What I care about

One thing I care about more and more is maintainability. I don't want to build something that only makes sense to me. If another developer looks at the project later, I want them to understand it easily. I also think about the client. They shouldn't need a developer to change some content or update data. In frontend work, I want that kind of flexibility to be part of the experience.

If I can build a project so it's easy to use and hard to break, that's a win for me. Clean code isn't about looking clever. It's about making the project easy to understand, update, and work with, especially on the frontend.

</section>

<section id="community">

## Building a community

While studying, I noticed that many students interested in development were learning on their own without really knowing others who shared the same interests. So I decided to create a development community myself; a place where students can connect, learn, and build together. It's still growing, but my goal is to eventually make it one of the biggest developer communities in Türkiye.

That's a big goal, I know. But I'd rather work toward it than decide it's too big.

</section>
